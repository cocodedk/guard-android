Guard for Android blocks dangerous sites and ads for every app on your phone, on any network: at home, on mobile data, on café Wi-Fi, abroad.

<b>How it works</b>
The app uses Android's VPN feature only as a local tunnel that ends inside the app. Only DNS lookups and connections to known dangerous addresses enter it. Names on the block list (the AdGuard DNS filter) get an empty answer; every other lookup is passed on to your network's own DNS server. Connections from any app to addresses on public lists of known dangerous addresses are refused at once, and a notification tells you which app tried. There is no VPN server: your other traffic goes out exactly as before.

<b>Private by design</b>
<ul>
<li>No account, no analytics, no ads and no server run by us.</li>
<li>The address lists are downloaded straight from the publishers to the phone, normally once a day, and only while protection runs. As with any visit to a website, the publishers can see your IP address; the app sends no information about you.</li>
<li>You grant only two permissions: the VPN connection and notifications. Network access is granted by Android automatically and is also used to download the address lists.</li>
<li>Free software under the GPL-3.0, free of charge.</li>
</ul>

<b>Says plainly when it stops</b>
If protection stops, for example because another VPN app took over, a notification says so. The app never fails silently.

<b>Made for everyone</b>
Built for TalkBack and large text from the start.

<b>Limits</b>
Apps with their own secure DNS bypass the name filter. Android's Private DNS set to a specific server can't be used with the app: protection stops and says so ("Automatic" works). Only one VPN app can run at a time. Address blocking needs the lists to be downloaded and under 7 days old; the screen says if a list is missing or paused.
