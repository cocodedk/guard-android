Guard for Android blocks dangerous sites and ads for every app on your phone, on any network: at home, on mobile data, on café Wi-Fi, abroad. The app is free and needs no account.

<b>What makes it different</b>
<ul>
<li><b>It blocks dangerous addresses too, without taking in all your traffic.</b> Some malicious software skips the name lookup and connects straight to an address. The app refuses connections to addresses on public lists of known dangerous addresses, such as criminal networks and botnet servers. Only DNS lookups and those connections enter the app. The rest of your traffic never does.</li>
<li><b>It tells you which app tried.</b> A notification names the app, for example "Chrome tried to connect to …", when Android can say which app it was. The Recent blocks list shows what each app tried to reach.</li>
<li><b>It does not stop silently.</b> If protection stops without your asking, a notification says that DNS lookups are no longer filtered. If an Android setting would cut the phone off from the internet, the app stops and explains why. If you turn off the app's notifications, it cannot tell you, and the screen reminds you of that.</li>
<li><b>Encrypted lookups stay encrypted.</b> Lookups that are not blocked are sent on by Android itself. If Android encrypts your lookups (Private DNS set to Automatic), they stay encrypted.</li>
<li><b>Nothing to set up.</b> One button and no lists to choose. Built for TalkBack from the start: every button has a spoken description, status changes are read aloud, and text can be enlarged to 200%.</li>
</ul>

<b>Getting started</b>
Open the app and tap Start protection. Android asks you for two permissions: the VPN connection and notifications. You stop protection with the Stop protection button.

<b>How it works</b>
<ul>
<li>When an app wants to open a site, the phone first asks where the site is. This is called a DNS lookup. If the name is on the block list (the AdGuard DNS filter, built into the app), the app gives an empty answer, and the site or ad is not loaded. Every other lookup goes on to your network's own DNS server.</li>
<li>The Recent blocks list exists only in the phone's memory and is cleared whenever protection starts or stops.</li>
</ul>

<b>What passes through the app</b>
The app uses Android's VPN feature, but only as a local tunnel that ends inside the app. There is no VPN server, and the rest of your traffic goes out as before. That is why Android shows a key in the status bar while protection runs.

<b>Privacy</b>
<ul>
<li>The app collects no information about you. No account, no analytics, no ads, and no server run by us.</li>
<li>The address lists are downloaded straight from the publishers to the phone, normally once a day and only while protection runs. As with any visit to a website, the publishers can see your IP address.</li>
<li>You grant only two permissions: the VPN connection and notifications. Android grants network access automatically, and it is also used to download the lists.</li>
<li>Free software under the GPL-3.0, and free of charge. Anyone can read the code.</li>
</ul>

<b>Limits</b>
<ul>
<li>Apps with their own secure DNS, for example a browser with DoH turned on, bypass the name filter.</li>
<li>Android's Private DNS set to a specific server can't be used with the app. The phone could not reach the internet, so protection stops and says so. "Automatic" works. For the same reason, Android's "Block connections without VPN" setting can't be used with the app either.</li>
<li>Android allows only one VPN app at a time, so the app can't run alongside another VPN.</li>
<li>Address blocking needs the lists to be downloaded and under 7 days old, because old lists can block addresses that others use now. The screen says if a list is missing or paused. The name filter works regardless.</li>
<li>No list catches everything.</li>
</ul>
