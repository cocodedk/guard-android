Free. No account. Open source.

Dangerous sites and ads are stopped inside your phone.

Cocode Guard for Android blocks dangerous sites and ads in every app, on any network: at home, on mobile data and on café Wi-Fi. It happens inside the phone. There is no account and no server.

Early version. The first release is still being built, and the download is coming soon. This page describes how the app is meant to work.

What it does

The app blocks known dangerous sites and ad servers for every app on the phone.

  • Every app

    Games, news apps and browsers are filtered the same way. You don't set anything up app by app.

  • Any network

    At home, on mobile data or on hotel Wi-Fi. The filter travels with the phone.

  • Free, with no account

    No payment, no login and no server run by us. The code is open under the GPL-3.0.

  • A well-known list

    The block list is AdGuard's DNS filter. It comes bundled in the app.

How it works

Here it is in plain words.

  1. The phone looks up a name

    Before an app can open a site, the phone has to find out where the site lives. It asks a DNS server, a bit like looking a number up in a phone book.

  2. The app listens in

    The lookup passes through the app first, inside the phone. If the name is on the block list, the app answers with nothing. The phone never learns where the site is, so it never opens.

  3. The rest is passed on

    Every other lookup goes on to the network's own DNS server, and the site opens as usual. Later you will be able to pick a different DNS server yourself.

Why does the app ask for VPN permission?

Android's VPN feature is how an ordinary app can see DNS lookups. The app uses it only as a local tunnel that starts and ends inside the app itself. There is no VPN server, and the rest of your traffic does not go through the app. The app sends nothing to us or anyone else. The DNS lookups go only to the network's own DNS server, just as before.

Privacy in short

  • No account and no server run by us.
  • No tracking, no statistics and no ads in the app.
  • Your DNS lookups go only to the DNS server the app uses. By default that is the network's own.
  • Counts of blocked sites stay on the phone.
  • You grant only two permissions: the VPN permission and notifications. Network access is granted by Android automatically. No access to contacts, location, files or Android's accessibility features.

What the app cannot do

It is better to know up front.

  • Android's Private DNS bypasses the filter

    If Private DNS is turned on in the phone's settings, the phone sends its lookups encrypted and around the app, so the app cannot see them. Turn Private DNS off if you want protection.

  • Apps with their own secure DNS bypass the filter

    Some apps and browsers look sites up in their own encrypted way. The app cannot see those lookups.

  • One VPN at a time

    Android allows only one VPN-type app at a time, so a work VPN cannot run alongside Cocode Guard.

  • No list catches everything

    The app blocks what is on the list. A new dangerous site can slip through, and a harmless site can be blocked by mistake.

  • If protection stops, you are told

    If protection stops, the app says so with a notification. It never stops in silence.

The Cocode Guard box

The app is the phone companion to the Cocode Guard box, the small box by the router that watches over the whole home and writes to you on Telegram. The box protects the house. The app protects the phone when it leaves the house. The app also works fine without the box.
