Free. No account. Sends nothing about you.

Dangerous sites and ads are stopped inside your phone.

Guard for Android blocks dangerous sites and ads in every app, on any network: at home, on mobile data and on café Wi-Fi. It happens inside the phone, and the app sends no information about you to anyone. There is no account and no server run by us. The app also refuses connections to known dangerous addresses.

Early version. The first release is still being built, and the download is coming soon. This page describes how the app is meant to work.

Good to know

  • The app sends no information about you to anyone. No account, no tracking, no analytics, no ads.
  • Everything happens on the phone. There is no Cocode server and no VPN server.
  • Only DNS lookups and connections to known dangerous addresses pass through the app. It never touches the rest of your traffic.
  • Your DNS lookups go only to the DNS server the app uses. By default that is the network's own.
  • Lists of dangerous addresses are downloaded straight from their publishers. They can see your IP address, as with any visit to a website.
  • You grant only two permissions: VPN and notifications. No access to contacts, location or files.
  • Free of charge and free software (GPL-3.0). Anyone can read the code.
  • Made for TalkBack and large text.

What it does

The app blocks known dangerous sites, ad servers and dangerous addresses for every app on the phone.

  • Every app

    Games, news apps and browsers are filtered the same way. You don't set anything up app by app.

  • Any network

    At home, on mobile data or on hotel Wi-Fi. The filter travels with the phone.

  • Dangerous addresses

    Some apps and some malicious software go straight to an address without looking up a name. The app refuses connections to addresses on public lists of known dangerous addresses, and tells you which app tried.

  • Free, with no account

    No payment, no login and no server run by us. The code is open under the GPL-3.0.

  • A well-known name list

    The names come from AdGuard's DNS filter. That list comes bundled in the app.

  • Public address lists

    The addresses come from public lists that the app downloads to the phone itself, straight from the people who publish them. Read about it in the privacy policy.

How it works

Here it is in plain words.

  1. The phone looks up a name

    Before an app can open a site, the phone has to find out where the site lives. It asks a DNS server, a bit like looking a number up in a phone book.

  2. The app listens in

    The lookup passes through the app first, inside the phone. If the name is on the block list, the app answers with nothing. The phone never learns where the site is, so it never opens.

  3. The rest is passed on

    Every other lookup goes on to the network's own DNS server, and the site opens as usual. Later you will be able to pick a different DNS server yourself.

And dangerous addresses?

Some apps and some malicious software do not ask for a name, but go straight to an address. So the app downloads lists of known dangerous addresses while protection runs. A connection to an address on the lists is refused inside the phone at once. A notification tells you which app tried and which address it was. All other connections go around the app.

Why does the app ask for VPN permission?

Android's VPN feature is how an ordinary app can see DNS lookups. The app uses it only as a local tunnel that starts and ends inside the app itself. There is no VPN server. Only DNS lookups and connections to addresses on the lists go through the app, and the rest of your traffic does not. The app sends nothing to us. The DNS lookups go only to the network's own DNS server, just as before, and the address lists are downloaded straight from the people who publish them.

What the app cannot do

It is better to know up front.

  • Private DNS must be Automatic or Off

    If Android's Private DNS is set to Automatic, the app works as normal and you need to do nothing. If Private DNS is set to a specific server (a host name), it cannot work together with the app. The phone would not reach the internet while protection runs, so the app stops protection and tells you. To use the app, choose Automatic or Off.

  • Apps with their own secure DNS bypass the filter

    Some apps and browsers look sites up in their own encrypted way. The app cannot see those lookups.

  • One VPN at a time

    Android allows only one VPN-type app at a time, so a work VPN cannot run alongside Guard for Android.

  • No list catches everything

    The app blocks what is on the lists. A new dangerous site or address can slip through, and a harmless site can be blocked by mistake.

  • Address blocking needs fresh lists

    The app downloads the address lists when the phone is online. If a list is missing, or is more than 7 days old, the app does not use it, and the screen says so. The name filter works regardless.

  • If protection stops, you are told

    If protection stops, the app says so with a notification. It never stops in silence.

The Cocode Guard box

The app is the phone companion to the Cocode Guard box, the small box by the router that watches over the whole home and writes to you on Telegram. The box protects the house. The app protects the phone when it leaves the house. The app also works fine without the box.
